## Narrator Script

Longevity isn't just about what you can do — it's about avoiding the injuries and accumulated damage that limit what you can do in the future. The Pain Log is where you track body signals: discomfort, soreness, acute pain, numbness, chronic patterns. Done consistently, it becomes one of the most useful datasets in the system.

---

### Why Log Pain

Most people ignore minor pain until it becomes a real injury. By that point, there was usually a warning period — a few weeks of minor signals that got dismissed. The Pain Log is where those signals go.

A single pain entry is just data. Ten entries over three weeks showing the same pattern — right knee, moderate intensity, after stairs — is actionable information. You can see it, name it, and address it before it becomes a forced rest.

Pain also changes during a day. A back that is a 2 in the morning and a 6 after three hours at a desk tells you something a single end-of-day number hides. That is why you can log as many entries a day as you need.

---

### Logging a Pain Entry

From the Engine dashboard, open **Pain Tracking**. Every time you press **Add entry**, a new entry is saved. Earlier entries are never overwritten.

---

### Pain Form Fields

**When** — The time you felt it. It starts at the current time and keeps up with the clock. Change it to log something from earlier in the day (or yesterday); press **Now** to go back to the current time.

**Physical discomfort (1–10)** — How strong it is.
- 1: No discomfort.
- 2–3: Mild. Slight soreness or stiffness.
- 4–7: Moderate to significant. Affects range of motion or performance.
- 8–10: Severe to acute. Hard to train through; see a professional if it stays.

**Affected locations** — Where it is. Pick every one that applies:
- Right Hip Flexor / Left Hip Flexor
- Right Glute / Left Glute
- SI Joint
- Lower Back (L5/S1)
- Mid Back (Thoracic)
- Neck
- Left Shoulder / Right Shoulder
- Left Hamstring
- Right Knee
- Left Hand / Right Hand
- Left Foot / Right Foot

Pick both sides when both hands or both feet are affected.

**Sensation type** — What it feels like:
- **Tightness** — tension, reduced range of motion
- **Pinching** — a sharp, catching feeling at one point
- **Dull Ache** — diffuse, low-grade discomfort
- **Sharp Stab** — acute, localized, stabbing
- **Burning** — heat, friction, nerve-like
- **Numbness** — reduced feeling, tingling, "pins and needles"

**Aggravating activities** (optional, one per line) — What you were doing: "Long drive", "Sitting with tablet (90 min)", "Stairs". This field is the most useful for pattern recognition.

**Notes** (optional) — Any other context: how long it has been there, whether it is getting better or worse, what helps.

Photos, voice notes, links and life categories on the form belong to the whole day, not to one entry.

---

### Today's Entries

Below the form, today's entries are listed newest first, with the day's highest intensity. Use the pencil to change an entry in place, or the bin to delete it.

If you are offline, the entry is still saved on your device. It shows **Waiting to sync** and is sent when you reconnect.

---

### Viewing Pain History

Press **Pain history** (or open Engine → History → Pain Tracking). Every entry is listed newest first, grouped by day, and each day shows its highest intensity.

Filter by:
- **From / To** — a date range
- **Intensity from / to** — for example only 6 and above
- **Location** — one location, or both sides of a pair, such as "Hand (left or right)"
- **Search notes** — words in your notes

The list keeps loading as you scroll, so you can go back as far as you have data. Edit or delete any entry right there. The chart at the top shows each day's highest intensity.

---

### How Your Day Is Summarized

Correlations and the AI weekly review read one pain summary per day: the day's highest intensity, every location, sensation and activity you logged that day, and all of the day's notes. It updates every time you add, edit or delete an entry.

Pain you logged before several entries a day were possible appears as one entry per day marked "From the daily log", with "Time not recorded".

---

### When to Log

Log pain when you notice it, not days later. Intensity ratings especially lose accuracy with time — what you remember as a 6 might have been a 4 at the time.

It doesn't need to be every minor ache. Use it when:
- Pain is noticeable enough to affect your workout, focus, or daily movement
- You've had the same discomfort two or more times
- You want a record to show a physical therapist or doctor ("here's the pattern over the last 6 weeks")

## Key Takeaways

- Every save adds a new entry: log pain as many times a day as you notice it
- The time starts at now and can be changed for something felt earlier
- Locations include hands and feet (left and right); sensations include Numbness
- Today's entries sit under the form with edit and delete; logging works offline
- Pain History lists every entry grouped by day, with date, intensity, location and notes filters and no 200-day limit
- Correlations and the AI weekly review use each day's highest intensity and everything logged that day
